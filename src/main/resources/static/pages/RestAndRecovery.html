Why Recovery Matters

Training provides the stimulus for change, but adaptation occurs during recovery. When you train, you apply stress to the body, causing micro-trauma to muscle fibers and fatiguing the central nervous system.

Without adequate recovery time, the body cannot repair itself or supercompensate (grow stronger). Chronic under-recovery leads to diminished performance, increased injury risk, and overtraining syndrome.

bedtime

Sleep and Recovery

Sleep is the most potent recovery tool available. During deep sleep, the body releases growth hormone, which is critical for tissue repair and muscle growth.

  • Aim for 7-9 hours of quality sleep per night.
  • Maintain a consistent sleep schedule.
  • Optimize your sleep environment (cool, dark, quiet).

Active Recovery

Active recovery involves light, low-intensity movement on rest days. It promotes blood flow, which helps clear metabolic waste products and delivers nutrients to recovering tissues without adding significant stress.

Examples

  • Brisk walking
  • Light cycling
  • Yoga / Mobility work
