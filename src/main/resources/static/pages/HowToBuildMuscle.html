How to Build Muscle

Understand the key training, nutrition, and recovery principles involved in muscle growth.

How Muscle Growth Works

Muscle growth, or hypertrophy, is the result of applying a specific stress to muscle fibers, causing micro-tears. In response, the body repairs these fibers, fusing them to form new muscle protein strands or myofibrils, which increase in thickness and number.

This adaptation stimulus is crucial. Without forcing the muscle beyond its current capacity, there is no biological necessity for growth. The central nervous system and metabolic pathways work in tandem to facilitate this process, provided the right conditions are met.

Resistance Training Pillars

fitness_center

Volume

The total amount of work performed (sets × reps × weight). Adequate volume is required to provide a sufficient growth stimulus, generally 10-20 hard sets per muscle group per week.

local_fire_department

Intensity

The effort level relative to your maximum capability. High intensity (lifting heavy relative to your 1RM) recruits high-threshold motor units essential for hypertrophy.

dashboard_customize

Exercise Selection

Choosing movements that effectively target the intended musculature across a full range of motion. A mix of compound and isolation exercises is optimal...

trending_up

Progressive Overload

Consistently increasing the demands on the musculoskeletal system over time (more weight, more reps, or better execution) to force continuous adaptation.

warning Training Close Enough to Failure

"Proximity to failure" refers to how many repetitions in reserve (RIR) you have before absolute muscular failure. Absolute failure is when you cannot complete another concentric rep despite maximal effort.

For hypertrophy, it is generally recommended to train most sets with 1-3 RIR. Training to absolute failure on every set creates disproportionate systemic fatigue, extending recovery times and potentially hindering total weekly volume. Save absolute failure for isolation movements or the final set of an exercise.

Recovery bedtime

Muscle is broken down in the gym and built in bed.

  • Sleep: 7-9 hours of quality sleep for hormone regulation (HGH release).
  • Rest Days: Necessary to prevent overtraining and systemic fatigue accumulation.
  • Stress Management: High cortisol can be catabolic (muscle-wasting).
